About

A builder, a student, and an operator.

I'm Taiwo Adebola, a cybersecurity student and aspiring software engineer based in Nigeria. The honest summary of what I do: I build things to understand them.

That habit started with small scripts and utilities and grew into the collection you can see in my projects — a church service planner with a live document preview, a Telegram bot with memory, a fintech prototype built around a proper ledger, and a self-hosted deployment environment I operate and troubleshoot myself. None of it is decorative. Each project exists because I wanted to know how that kind of system actually works, and building it was the only honest way to find out.

The thread connecting it all is a pull toward the layer beneath the layer — what the browser actually sends, what the proxy actually does with it, what the database actually records. My study of cybersecurity sharpened that instinct into a discipline: understand the system before you trust it, and describe the system accurately before you claim anything about it.

I distinguish carefully between what I've built and what I'm becoming. I'm not yet a professional engineer with years of production experience — I'm a serious student of the craft with a growing portfolio of real, working projects and the discipline to label them accurately. What I can offer today is genuine curiosity, honest engineering, and the habit of finishing what I start.

What I'm interested in

Interests, not endorsements — each of these shows up in real projects, at varying stages of depth.

Software engineering & full-stack development

Applications end to end — data model, API, interface. The projects I keep returning to are document tools, planning systems and this website.

Web & mobile application development

Interfaces that respect the user's device and attention. Mobile-first layouts, honest loading states, and interactions that never block the task.

Cybersecurity & security research

My field of study. I approach it as a builder — inspection tools and verification pipelines that teach me how systems expose themselves.

Artificial intelligence & machine learning

Intelligence applied narrowly: bots that remember, agents with bounded actions, and automation that removes repetition without removing judgement.

DevOps, cloud infrastructure & deployment

Running real infrastructure myself — Coolify, Docker, PostgreSQL, Redis, EC2 over SSH. Deployment as practiced skill, not theory.

Linux, open source & automation

Linux as daily driver and lab. Open source as both toolchain and curriculum — reading other people's code is half the education.

APIs, bots, integrations & developer tooling

The connective tissue of software — Telegram bots, GitHub integrations, CLI utilities, and the small tools that make daily work lighter.

Data & systems thinking

Ledgers over balances, evidence over verdicts, inventory before defence. How data is shaped decides what the system can honestly do.

How I work

    01

    Build to understand

    I don't trust knowledge I can't reconstruct. Reading about DNS taught me less than one evening of broken records did.

    02

    Say what it actually does

    Concept, in development, working — the labels matter. My portfolio keeps them distinct because credibility is the whole product.

    03

    Boring is a feature

    Logs before restarts. One variable at a time. The unglamorous discipline is what keeps systems alive.

    04

    Leave the judgements to humans

    Automation should absorb repetition, not responsibility. My bots report; my tools flag leads; the conclusions stay mine to make.
